Privacy

1. What We Collect

MonSTAR creates your account the first time you sign in with Google. Google sends your email address, account ID, name and profile photo. We keep:

  • Your Monash email address, to limit sign-in to Monash accounts and find your account each time you sign in. Only you and admins can see it.
  • Your Google account ID, to link your Google account to your MonSTAR account. No one can see it.
  • Your username, which shows who wrote a review. It starts as your authcate (the part of your student email before the @), or your first name if you use a staff email. It's public.
  • Your profile picture: a link to your Google profile photo, or a picture you upload. It's public and shows next to your reviews.
  • An admin flag, which grants moderation rights. Only you and admins can see it.
  • The reviews you liked and disliked, so you can vote once per review. Only you and admins can see this list.
  • Your notifications, which tell you who liked your reviews. Only you can see them.
  • A hash of your session token, which keeps you signed in. No one can see it.

We discard the name on your Google account. We don't ask for phone numbers, postal addresses, student ID numbers, dates of birth or payment details.

Because your username starts as your authcate, anyone who can search the Monash directory can find out who wrote your reviews until you change it. You can change your username and upload a different picture on your profile page.

Reviews. A review holds a title, your review text, the semester and year you took the unit, an optional grade (HD, D, C, P or N), four ratings from 0 to 5, like and dislike counts, and when you wrote and last edited it. Reviews are public and show your username and profile picture.

Votes. Like and dislike counts are public, but the list of reviews you voted on is private. When you like a review, its author gets a notification with your username and profile picture. Dislikes send no notification.

Analytics. MonSTAR uses Vercel Web Analytics to count page views. It sets no cookies. For each page view Vercel records the time, the page address, the referring site, a location derived from the request (country, region and city), and your device type, operating system and browser. Profile page addresses reach Vercel with the username replaced by a placeholder. Vercel tells visitors apart with a hash of the request, discards that hash after 24 hours, and shows us only aggregate figures.

Cookies and browser storage. The site sets two cookies, both for sign-in (section 4). It keeps your unit list preferences, such as sort order and filters, in your browser's local storage, not on our servers.

2. Where Your Data Lives

  • MongoDB Atlas (AWS Sydney) is our main database and holds everything above.
  • Vercel (Sydney) serves the site, runs our API and counts page views. It handles all API traffic in transit, server logs and page-view analytics.
  • Cloudinary (United States) stores the profile pictures you upload.
  • Google Sign-In confirms who is signing in. It sends us your email address, account ID, name and photo when you sign in.
  • Google Gemini writes the AI overview on each unit page. It gets each unit's review titles, text, semesters, years, grades and ratings, but no usernames, emails or account IDs.
  • Upstash Redis (AWS Sydney) caches popular units and the jobs board. It holds review titles, text and ratings for the most-reviewed units, without author details.

Review text is whatever its author wrote, so it can mention people. Nothing sends reviews to Gemini automatically: a developer regenerates the overviews by hand. We use Gemini's free tier, where Google's terms let Google use prompts to improve its products and let human reviewers read them.

The site redirects plain HTTP to HTTPS and tells browsers to use HTTPS on later visits. Atlas requires TLS on every database connection and encrypts stored data and snapshots with AES-256.

3. Who Can See Your Data

  • Anyone can read reviews, like and dislike counts, and public profiles without signing in. A public profile shows a username, a profile picture and that user's reviews. Public pages and the public API show no account data beyond usernames and profile pictures.
  • You, when signed in, can see your own email address, votes and notifications. You can change your username and profile picture, edit or delete your reviews, and delete your account. You can't change anyone else's data.
  • Admins moderate the site. They can list every user, including email addresses, change usernames, and edit or delete any review or account. The server checks admin rights on every admin request.
  • People who manage MonSTAR's service accounts on Atlas, Vercel, Cloudinary, Upstash and Google Cloud can reach the data those services hold.

4. How We Protect It

  • Sign-in. Google handles sign-in. Our server checks that each Google token is genuine and was issued for MonSTAR, then rejects any email address that isn't a Monash student or staff address. MonSTAR has no password sign-in, and no account holds a password.
  • Sessions. After you sign in, the server sets two cookies. The access token identifies you on each request and expires after 15 minutes. The refresh token lasts 180 days and gets you a new access token without signing in again. The database stores only a SHA-256 hash of the refresh token. Each refresh swaps the token for a new one, and signing out deletes the stored hash. Both cookies are HttpOnly (page scripts can't read them), Secure (browsers send them only over HTTPS) and SameSite=Strict (browsers don't send them with requests that start on other sites).
  • Permissions. Only a review's author or an admin can edit or delete it, and only you or an admin can change your username or delete your account.
  • Secrets. API keys and the token-signing secret live in Vercel's environment settings. Our public code holds only placeholders.
  • Breaches. If we suspect a breach, we contain it first, then work out what data was exposed. We tell the Monash Student Association within 72 hours of confirming a breach, and email affected users as soon as we know who they are.

5. How Long We Keep It

We keep your account, reviews, votes and notifications until you delete them or your account. Only sessions expire on their own: access tokens after 15 minutes and refresh tokens after 180 days.

Some copies outlast the original:

  • The popular-units cache holds review text and ratings, without author details, for up to one week.
  • Each unit's AI overview summarises the reviews the unit had when a developer last regenerated it. A deleted review still shapes that summary until the next regeneration.
  • Our server logs internal user IDs, not names or emails, when it deletes an account. Vercel keeps runtime logs for one hour.

The database has no backups, so deleted data leaves no copy behind.

Deleting your account. Use the Delete account button on your profile page, then confirm. MonSTAR then:

  1. deletes all your reviews and recalculates the average ratings of the units you reviewed;
  2. deletes the notifications addressed to you;
  3. removes your likes and dislikes from other people's reviews;
  4. deletes your uploaded profile picture, if you uploaded one;
  5. deletes your account record.

Steps 1 to 3 run in one database transaction, so if any of them fails, MonSTAR deletes nothing and keeps your account. Notifications you caused by liking other people's reviews survive the deletion. They stay in those people's inboxes, with your username and picture link.

6. Seeing, Correcting and Deleting Your Data

  • See it. When signed in, you can see your profile, reviews and notifications on the site. For a full copy, email monstarapp@gmail.com from your Monash address. We reply within 30 days with your account record, reviews and notifications.
  • Correct it. Change your username and picture on your profile page, and edit your reviews at any time.
  • Delete it. Delete single reviews, or your whole account (section 5).
